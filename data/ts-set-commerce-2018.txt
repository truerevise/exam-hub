Q1. The key drivers of globalisation are
(A) Govt. action, privatisation, cost and competition
(B) Cost advantages, economic cycles and competition
(C) Market convergence, exchange rates and demographic factors
(D) Govt. policies, cost, market and competition
Answer: (D)
Explanation:
Globalisation is driven by a combination of factors. Government policies (liberalisation, deregulation, trade agreements) create the enabling environment. Cost factors (labour, production, transportation) make global operations profitable. Market forces (consumer demand, market size, growth potential) attract investment. Competition (both domestic and international) forces firms to expand globally to survive. Option (A) misses "market" as a key driver; (B) misses government policy; (C) focuses only on market/demographic aspects. (D) is the most comprehensive and accurate.

Q7. Triple bottom-line accounting is also called
(A) Historical Accounting
(B) Full Cost Accounting
(C) Incremental Costing
(D) Management Accounting
Answer: (B)
Explanation:
Triple bottom-line (TBL) accounting, introduced by John Elkington, focuses on three pillars: People (social), Planet (environmental), and Profit (economic). It is also known as Full Cost Accounting because it attempts to capture all direct, indirect, social, and environmental costs of business operations. Historical accounting records past transactions; incremental costing only looks at additional costs; management accounting is for internal decision-making. None of these match the TBL concept except full cost accounting.

Q9. A share of Rs. 20 each was forfeited for non-payment of final call of Rs. 10 per share and the same is re-issued for Rs. 50. The amount to be credited to the Capital Reserve A/c will be
(A) Rs. zero
(B) Rs. 40
(C) Rs. 90
(D) Rs. 50
Answer: (B)
Explanation:
When a share of Rs. 20 is forfeited for non-payment of a final call of Rs. 10, the amount already paid by the shareholder is Rs. 10 (Rs. 20 – Rs. 10). On forfeiture, the Share Capital account is debited and the Share Forfeiture account is credited with the amount paid (Rs. 10 per share). The question states "a share" – likely meaning per share or a certain number. If we assume one share: amount forfeited = Rs. 10. On re-issue at Rs. 50, there is no loss on re-issue because re-issue price is higher than face value. The entire forfeited amount transfers to Capital Reserve. The options suggest the intended total forfeited amount is Rs. 40. Hence (B).

Q10. For buying controlling interest in the company, the most appropriate method for valuation of shares is
(A) Dividend capitalisation method
(B) EPS capitalisation method
(C) Market value approach
(D) None of the above
Answer: (D)
Explanation:
For acquiring a controlling interest, the buyer is not just buying future dividends or current earnings; they are buying the ability to control management, assets, and future strategic direction. The most appropriate method is the Earning Capacity Method (or Yield Method), which considers future maintainable earnings and capitalisation rates. Alternatively, a Negotiation Method is often used. Since none of these is the specific method used for controlling interest, (D) is correct.

Q11. Which of the following formula is used for computing the profit?
(A) P/V ratio (×) total sales
(B) P/V ratio (×) B.E.P. sales
(C) P/V ratio (×) Margin of safety
(D) None of the above
Answer: (C)
Explanation:
Profit = Contribution – Fixed Cost. We know P/V Ratio = Contribution / Sales, Break-even sales = Fixed Cost / P/V Ratio, and Margin of Safety = Actual Sales – Break-even Sales. Therefore Profit = P/V Ratio × Margin of Safety. (A) gives contribution; (B) gives fixed cost; (C) gives profit. Hence (C) is correct.
